# FlyBrain V1.19.1 — locomotion audit (2026-09-28)

## Scope and release integrity

Audited the supplied V1.19.1 source snapshot, with focus on FBR-10/FBC103, the six-leg actuator, VNC motor decoding, body integration, wall contact, wing motor readout, and available automated checks. This document records a source audit; it is not a claim of biological validation or a successful Android release build.

## Confirmed findings

### 1. Six-leg ground actuator is present and causally separated

`MainActivity` derives six group-specific leg motor rates, estimates a per-group tonic baseline, extracts phasic excess/rise, and passes those six activations plus measured walk-OFF activity to `LeggedSensorimotorActuator.step()`. Food, sensory stimulus, and action-selection variables are not arguments to that actuator. Body position and heading are integrated from actuator velocity/yaw. Wall contact constrains outward velocity and feeds wall pressure back to the actuator.

`LeggedSensorimotorActuator` contains six leg phases, stance/swing, contact/load, foot trajectories, support estimates, propulsion, lateral motion, and yaw. Its current gait is an imposed modified-tripod mechanical coordination pattern; it is not a complete reconstruction of the fly's distributed central pattern generator or muscle/tendon mechanics.

### 2. Confirmed major gap: wings do not produce physical flight

The measured wing and jump motor outputs are converted into `wingActivity`/`jumpActivity`. `flightTarget`, `flightFactor`, and `flightPhase` are updated, but the flight state is not coupled to body translation, vertical position, lift, pitch/roll, or a flight-mode transition. `wingBeatPhase` is used for wing animation. Therefore the present implementation can animate wingbeats but cannot produce neural-output-driven takeoff or aerial locomotion. This is a code-path finding, not a tuning hypothesis.

### 3. Ground locomotion remains a reduced phenomenological actuator

The body is a 2D point-like state with bounded forward/lateral speed and yaw. Stance propulsion is a normalized proxy derived from foot-stride rate; yaw is driven by left/right propulsion imbalance. The actuator does not model individual joint torques, body mass/inertia, ground reaction forces, slip, or measured Drosophila gait kinematics. Its constants and tripod offsets are model assumptions, not calibrated from a cited kinematic dataset in this repository.

The current yaw sign convention is encoded by `turnBalance = rightPropulsion - leftPropulsion`, and the unit test explicitly expects stronger right-side output to produce positive yaw. That convention should be validated against the renderer's heading coordinate system and empirical turning kinematics before changing it; blindly flipping it could reintroduce the prior directional bias.

### 4. FBR-10/FBC103

The bundled FBC103 structural artifact passes the repository's reader self-test (16,669 nodes; 26-byte records; SHA-256 `0044ab166af3439f2b86d4e6c5897481a1c3f28a58b6afb2c4f761489b276bbf`). The snapshot does not include the canonical generated FBD105 dynamics artifact or the runtime sensory/VNC TSV maps listed in `AUDIT_REMAINING.md`; these are expected to be generated by CI from pinned MaleCNS inputs. Consequently, this local ZIP alone cannot establish that the exact packaged runtime graph and all motor mappings match the canonical build outputs.

No FBR-10/FBC103 neuron or edge was altered in this audit. The available evidence does not justify editing the frozen connectome; the confirmed wing/body disconnection is downstream in runtime mechanics.

## Conclusion / next engineering boundary

V1.19.1 has a coherent, causally separated *ground-leg* actuator, but this audit cannot certify realistic locomotion. The clearest confirmed locomotor omission is physical flight: wing/jump motor output is currently presentation-only with respect to body motion. A scientifically defensible next change needs an explicit flight state and 2D/3D mechanics contract, validated motor-role mapping for wing steering/lift, and tests for no-motor/no-flight, bilateral wing output, asymmetric wing output, takeoff/landing transitions, and conservation/bounds. Until that is implemented and validated against reference behavior, describe wings as animated, not locomotor.
