# FlyBrain V1.19.5 — wall-stuck root cause and correction

## Confirmed root cause
The previous wall reflex scaled escape drive by:
`forwardIntoWall = inwardSpeed / MAX_FORWARD_SPEED`.

In the attached recording, after the fly reaches the right boundary, the displayed
physical speed falls close to zero while wall contact remains present. That means the
escape drive also collapses toward zero exactly when a persistent turn is most needed.
The body can therefore continue trying to propel into a clamped boundary.

The previous implementation also selected the escape side from contact-state pressure
that itself depended on the already-collapsed speed, and it applied the new escape yaw
on the following actuator step.

## V1.19.5 correction
- Contact is now an explicit mechanical state passed every frame.
- Escape direction is latched once per contact episode.
- Escape strength is based primarily on body orientation relative to the wall normal,
  not on translational speed.
- The yaw actuator responds immediately through its existing first-order dynamics.
- Penetration velocity is removed while tangential slip is preserved.
- A short propulsion unload occurs only for a face-on impact.
- Boundary sensing begins slightly earlier (.014 normalized units) so the reflex has
  room to develop before the body is hard-clamped.
- No random steering, no direct heading assignment, no food/light/danger variable
  enters the actuator.

Expected behavior: a fly arriving at a wall and slowing to zero should still rotate
away from it and leave the boundary rather than repeatedly push into the clamp.
