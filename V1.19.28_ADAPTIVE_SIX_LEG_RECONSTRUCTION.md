# FlyBrain V1.19.28 — Adaptive six-leg mechanics reconstruction

## Purpose

This release begins the locomotion reconstruction requested after the V1.19.27 video review. The confirmed V1.19.1 audit described the previous actuator as an imposed modified-tripod mechanical coordination pattern rather than a complete reconstruction of distributed locomotor coordination.

## Changes

- The six leg phases remain independent state variables driven by their measured neural motor activations.
- The permanent per-frame `TRIPOD_OFFSETS` constraint has been removed. The previous tripod values are used only as deterministic startup phases so the fly does not begin with all six legs phase-aligned.
- The actuator now computes a signed yaw-torque proxy from the force of each stance foot and its actual lateral body position, instead of reducing the six legs to a single bilateral scalar before computing yaw.
- The bilateral propulsion imbalance remains available as a bounded fallback for numerical cases where the individual torque proxy is near zero.
- No wall, food, danger, approach-action, or target variable enters the actuator.
- FBR-10/FBC103 is not modified.

## What this does NOT claim

This is the first mechanical reconstruction step, not a claim of biologically complete Drosophila locomotion. Joint-level muscle dynamics, true VNC CPG reconstruction, stance/swing control from identified premotor circuits, and calibrated kinematics remain open.

## Acceptance target

The next APK experiment should determine whether the six independent neural leg streams now produce variable inter-leg timing and whether wall contact produces a measurable change in the downstream motor pattern rather than only a physical velocity clamp.
