# FlyBrain V1.19.36 — retained TURN-DN yaw + arena visual closure

## Observed failure
V1.19.35 could translate along the arena boundaries, but the rendered fly often remained head-first into the wall. The body mechanics had no direct physical interface for the retained bilateral descending neurons labelled `TURN`: their activity was used by action/diagnostic readouts, while yaw was generated only indirectly from six-leg force imbalance.

## Correction
V1.19.36 measures actual role-2 descending-neuron spikes over every 40 × 0.5 ms neural substep in the 20 ms frame, resolves them by the frozen FBR-10 soma-side metadata, normalizes the rate per retained neuron, applies a short activation filter, and supplies the result to the mechanical actuator as a bilateral neural yaw signal.

The actuator combines two bounded causal paths:

`retained TURN-DN output -> bilateral neural yaw`  
`retained LEG-MN output -> stance force asymmetry -> mechanical yaw`

Opposing left/right turn activity cancels. Equal left/right turn activity produces no yaw. TURN-DN output can rotate the body even when forward translation is zero, which is necessary to escape a wall-facing deadlock without writing a direct heading command.

## Arena visual closure
The previous runtime treated the white arena as visually empty unless the user enabled
LIGHT or DANGER. That made a fly at a boundary mechanically collide with a wall while
its visual receptor populations received no boundary information. V1.19.36 therefore
adds a bounded five-ray per-eye arena-wall visual field derived from body position and
heading. This field is injected only into the retained left/right primary visual
receptors; it then has to traverse the retained connectome and reach the measured
TURN-DN population. There is no direct wall-to-yaw or wall-to-heading operation.

At the centre of the arena the boundary cue is weak; it rises smoothly as a wall enters
the eye's visual sector. Independent Poisson sampling of the left/right receptor rates
provides the permitted neural variability, so a symmetric wall encounter does not
contain a hard-coded turn direction.
