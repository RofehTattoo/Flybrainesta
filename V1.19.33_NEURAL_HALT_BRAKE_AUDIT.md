# FlyBrain V1.19.33 — Neural Halt / BRK Audit

## Purpose

V1.19.33 is a controlled correction on top of V1.19.32_REGRESSION_RECOVERY. The goal is to restore a missing causal path for experimentally identified halting neurons without changing FBR-10 selection, the olfactory route, sensory encoders, or adding any environment-to-motor shortcut.

## Root cause addressed

The runtime already measured two distinct halting mechanisms:

- FG / BB (`halt_role` 1/2): walk-OFF populations.
- BRK (`halt_role` 3): VNC brake population.

V1.19.32 computed `haltBrakeSpikeEventsFrame` and `brakeNow`, but `applyMechanicalBodyState()` passed only `walkOffActivationState` to the mechanical actuator. Therefore the retained BRK neural output was diagnostic only and could not produce its distinct mechanical consequence.

## V1.19.33 correction

`LeggedSensorimotorActuator.step()` now accepts three independent neural inputs:

1. six measured VNC leg motor group activations;
2. measured FG/BB walk-OFF activation;
3. measured BRK brake activation.

BRK is converted from measured spike rate into a short first-order activation state in `MainActivity`, using the same measured-neural pipeline as walk-OFF. The actuator applies BRK as an independent gait gate and braking acceleration. No food, wall, danger, approach action, or software behaviour flag is passed into the actuator.

The code deliberately does not merge BRK into walk-OFF, because the two mechanisms have different semantics.

## Secondary locomotor correction

During audit of the existing six-leg mechanics, symmetric motor output produced a persistent yaw bias because the instantaneous foot-force moment converted the alternating tripod phase into a non-zero mean steering signal. V1.19.33 changes the steering moment to the bilateral net stance-force imbalance and filters the resulting torque briefly. This preserves sustained left/right neural asymmetry while preventing symmetric gait phasing from becoming a permanent heading bias.

No random steering, wall-turn command, food-turn command, or direct position write was introduced.
